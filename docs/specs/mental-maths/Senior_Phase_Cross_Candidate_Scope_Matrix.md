## Cross-cutting patterns visible only at this consolidated level

1. **Every candidate in this matrix shows the same shape**: CAPS names
   sub-constructs separately (by bullet, by list item, or by numbered
   topic), while this project's legacy candidate names bundle some or
   all of them together, with no affirmative CAPS or governance
   statement located anywhere in this matrix that requires the bundle.
   This is not a coincidence specific to any one candidate — it is a
   pattern across all five, and may itself be worth naming as a
   standing finding before any individual decisions are made.

2. **Two distinct kinds of unresolved overlap exist**, and they should
   not be treated the same way:
   - **Shared-sentence overlaps** (addSub ↔ mulDivFluency): the exact
     same CAPS worked material is cited as evidence for two different
     candidates.
   - **Shared-mechanism-family overlaps** (fracDecPercent ↔
     powersRootsFluency): different CAPS material, evidencing the same
     underlying mathematical mechanism (squares/cubes/roots) applied to
     different number types (integers vs. common/decimal fractions).
   
   These may warrant different resolutions — a shared sentence might
   argue for merging two candidates into one; a shared mechanism-family
   applied to different number types might instead argue for a
   deliberate "extends to" relationship between two candidates that
   remain separate. This matrix does not resolve either.

3. **Only one candidate (`ratioSharing`) involves an *already-existing*
   split with no located justification**, as opposed to an *unmade*
   bundling/splitting decision. This is a different kind of open
   question — the other four candidates ask "should we split/bundle,"
   while ratioSharing asks "was the existing split ever justified,"
   and its resolution may need to look for a lost document rather than
   new CAPS evidence.

4. **fracDecPercent is now the structurally richest candidate in this
   matrix** (4 CAPS-named topics, 2 confirmed cross-candidate
   overlaps — one via shared mechanism family, contributed by its own
   G9 material) — a materially different shape than it had before the
   deeper evidence pass, when it looked like a 3-topic candidate with
   1 overlap.

## Non-inference constraints in force

- This matrix draws only on already-committed evidence and scope-
  decision documents (see the repository-record caveat above for which
  candidates that includes) plus, where noted, uploaded-but-uncommitted
  content from this working session. It performs no new CAPS
  extraction of its own.
- No cell in this matrix should be read as a taxonomy decision. The
  "affirmative evidence to bundle/split" rows report what evidence
  exists, not what should be concluded from it.
- CAPS's own topic-numbering structure (e.g. "1.4" vs. "1.5") is
  recorded as observed structural evidence, not treated as
  automatically decisive — consistent with this project's standing
  posture that named-topic structure informs but does not resolve
  scope questions on its own.
- The absence of a committed scope-decision review for
  powersRootsFluency, ratioSharing, and addSub must not be read as
  those candidates being less "open" than mulDivFluency or
  fracDecPercent — their OPEN status is unchanged; only the repository
  paper trail differs.
- No taxonomy status, Generation Policy, implementation, or test
  change is authorized by this document.
