## Evidence boundary in force for this decision

Only `powersRootsFluency_Evidence_Review_Checkpoint.md` is treated as
authoritative. That checkpoint established named-and-worked evidence
at every Senior Phase grade (G7/G8/G9), under the CAPS topic
"Exponents" (1.2), comprising four distinguishable sub-constructs:

1. **Squares/cubes and their roots** — G7 floor stated ("squares to at
   least 12²", "cubes to at least 6³"), worked examples and
   misconception guards present; extended to rational numbers at G8
   ("(0,7)² = 0,49").
2. **Exponential form / notation** — G7: "aᵇ = a × a × a × ... for b
   number of factors," with worked examples; extended to integers and
   rationals at G8, including a resultant-sign rule for negative bases.
3. **Laws of exponents** — introduced at G8 (product, quotient, power,
   distributive, zero-exponent laws, each worked), extended to integer
   exponents (including negative exponents, aᵐ = 1/aᵐ) at G9.
4. **Scientific notation** — introduced at G8 (positive exponents),
   extended to negative exponents at G9.

All four sit under the single named CAPS topic "Exponents," but are
introduced at different grades, with different evidence shapes: (1)
and (2) are named-and-worked from G7; (3) does not appear until G8;
(4) does not appear until G8. G7 has no scientific-notation or
general-laws content at all.

**Excluded from this decision:** any existing powers/roots code
implementation, and the numeric-ceiling gaps already flagged as
outstanding in the evidence checkpoint (those are a separate,
unresolved question from the scope/bundling question addressed here).

## Which of A / B / C is supported

**C — the scope question remains explicitly OPEN**, for a reason
distinct from `mulDivFluency` and `addSub`: here the four sub-
constructs are not evidenced unevenly in *quality* (all four are
named-and-worked wherever they appear) but unevenly in *grade
coverage* — a G7 item drawing only on squares/cubes/exponential form
would test different content than a G9 item drawing on integer
exponent laws and negative scientific notation, even though both
would currently be generated under the same candidate name.

- **A** (keep bundled as one candidate) is not clearly supported:
  nothing in CAPS states these four sub-constructs must be treated as
  one generation-eligible unit; "Exponents" is a topic heading, not a
  statement that all sub-content is interchangeable across grades.
- **B** (split into per-sub-construct or per-grade-tier candidates) is
  not clearly supported either: CAPS presents them under one
  continuous topic with explicit continuity language ("learners
  consolidate... developed in Grade 8"), which cuts against treating
  them as unrelated candidates.
- **C** is the only position consistent with the evidence actually in
  hand: bundling is plausible (one CAPS topic, continuous
  development) and splitting is plausible (four distinguishable
  sub-constructs, uneven grade introduction), and no governance
  decision or CAPS statement resolves which framing should govern
  item generation.
